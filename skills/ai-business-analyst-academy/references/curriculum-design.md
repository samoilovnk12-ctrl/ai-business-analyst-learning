# Curriculum design

Use this workflow when creating or revising the program, competency map, sequence, assessments, simulation, or lesson standard.

## Backward design

Start from observable end performance, not from a list of topics. For every competency, define the real work, acceptable evidence, prerequisites, minimum theory, guided practice, independent transfer, later retrieval, controlling sources, and where it is taught, practiced, assessed, and reused.

Separate `must perform`, `must explain`, and `useful awareness`. Do not give every roadmap item equal weight.

## Program layers

Maintain connected layers for business analysis; process and requirements; data and metrics; systems and delivery; AI capabilities; AI opportunity economics, risk, pilots and adoption; and career, portfolio and consulting.

Use external roadmaps as coverage checks, not as the order of instruction.

## Learning architecture evidence

Subject-matter sources answer `what is true`. Learning-science and instructional-design sources answer `how to teach it`. Audit both groups. Convert approved evidence into a course standard covering outcomes, worked examples, retrieval, spacing, interleaving, feedback, cognitive load, transfer, and assessment.

## Simulation contract

The company story supplies realistic context and consequences. Each event must exercise an already explained or deliberately introduced skill. Provide concrete people, data, documents, time period, constraints, and decision stakes. Avoid artificial prompts with an obviously foolish proposed solution. Mistakes create recoverable learning consequences, not dead ends.

## Release method

Design the competency and assessment spine first. Build one complete module, have the learner use it, and revise the standard from observed problems before producing the remaining lessons.
