# Source research

Use this workflow when adding or reassessing a book, article, video, course, standard, community, or board of resources.

## Evidence states

Record one honest state for every source:

1. `discovered` — title or link is known.
2. `accessible` — legal access to the required content was confirmed.
3. `structurally-reviewed` — author, edition/date, audience, table of contents, and format were inspected.
4. `deep-read` — the relevant content was read or watched, with chapter or section notes.
5. `cross-checked` — important claims were compared with primary or independent high-trust sources.
6. `approved`, `supplemental`, or `rejected` — its role in the course was decided and explained.

Never describe a source as studied when only its title, thumbnail, abstract, or table of contents was inspected.

## Source card

Capture the stable identifier; title, author, edition, date, and language; legal access; evidence state; audience; outline; claims; methods; examples; exercises; supported skills; reliability; relevance; freshness; clarity; practicality; Russian accessibility; limitations; course decision; and verifiable citations or locations.

Use short paraphrases. Do not reproduce copyrighted chapters, transcripts, or substantial excerpts.

## Trust hierarchy

Prefer current laws, standards, official documentation, original research, and primary evidence; then recognized textbooks and professional bodies; then experienced practitioners with transparent methods; then explanatory articles and videos. Treat opinion, marketing, or anonymous material only as examples.

A clear video is not automatically authoritative. A primary source is not automatically a good lesson. Use authoritative sources to establish truth and strong educators to explain it.

## Books and subscription readers

Ask for title and edition first. If the user wants to use a signed-in reader such as Yandex Books, the user signs in personally; never request credentials. Read only content made available in the authorized session. Do not bypass DRM, export restrictions, paywalls, or anti-automation controls. If full text cannot be accessed, keep the state below `deep-read` and request a legal alternative.

## Integration gate

Do not add a source to a required lesson until the learning outcome is named, evidence is sufficient, conflicts and freshness risks are understood, required learner material is available in Russian or explained in Russian, workload fits, and the source adds value beyond approved material.

Keep `RESOURCES.md` small. Store exhaustive research and rejected candidates in the workspace research catalog.
